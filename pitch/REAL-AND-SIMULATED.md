# What is real and what is simulated

Rewritten 2026-11-15 (after the stadium, the announcer and the Scout changes) from the code on `main` and the DONE posts on the board, and corrected the same day after the switch to real data (the games the app plays, the real soccer match, the voice). If something is not listed as real, assume it is not. Items we could not check are marked.

## The demo week (D54): what is real now

The league's current week is week 9 of the 2026 NFL season, played by real players. The app opens on it: `data/games/current.js` is `week09-gob-tur`, and the screens say Week 9.

- The featured game is `data/games/week09-gob-tur.json`: Goblins against Turtles, built from every catch and run the rostered players really made in NFL week 9 (ESPN play-by-play), with their real yards and touchdowns. 204 plays, 27 to 30 on the field, 106.3 to 108.7 in fantasy points (the Turtles win). The game maker ran without Jev.
- The league has three real weeks. Weeks 7 and 8 are finished (four games, `week07-gob-owl.json`, `week07-pyt-tur.json`, `week08-gob-pyt.json`, `week08-tur-owl.json`, built the same way from those weeks' real plays); their scores and the standings (Goblins 2 and 0) are the fantasy points of those games. Week 9 is current and its points are not in the league file.
- Proof that the points are the real week's: `node data/checks/real-week.js` compares the game's points for every running back, receiver and tight end with the points the scoring rules give his real stat line from Sleeper for week 9 (`data/statlines/week09-gob-tur-real.json`, `week09-pyt-owl-real.json`). 19 players, all within 0.25 points (mostly equal). Quarterbacks and kickers are not forced to match and the check prints each difference: a quarterback's passing in the game is only the catches by our own receivers (Allen 25.5 in the game, 12.9 in his real line; Jackson did not play, real line 0), and a kicker's extra points follow the game's touchdowns. Garrett Wilson has no real week 9 line (he did not play), so he is not compared.
- Still made up: the league itself (the teams, who opted in, that these four managers play each other in this order), every drive start, incompletion and penalty (the maker's filler), the order of the plays, the clock and the quarters (the files carry none), and the defenses. The quarterback's yards are not his real passing yards. The league note says so.

## Real

- The app runs in a browser with no install and no build. No frameworks.
- The football rules engine (`js/rules.js`) works out downs, distance, possession, first downs, touchdowns, turnovers on downs, four kinds of penalty and the score from the plays. `node tools/validate.js` checks the sample game against it.
- The pixel players and the field are drawn in code, not from image files.
- Hidden scores: when a manager opts in, points and stat lines stay masked until the final, then the recap shows the winner, top performers, the story of the game and the scoring plays. Checked in headless Chromium at phone width. Not checked on a real phone.
- The speed control and the floating fantasy points (they can be switched off).
- The announcer writes a line for each play in one style, plus a pre-game intro, a halftime report that leaves out fantasy points while scores are hidden, a full-time wrap and big calls for the top plays. There is no Spanish voice: the Spanish switch translates the screens only, and the announcer's captions stay English. ElevenLabs recordings (744 football lines in the voice Ninja, 282 soccer lines in Nathaniel, 14.8 MB, in `assets/voice/`) cover every line, catch-up lines after a jump included, of the football games `week09-gob-tur`, `week09-pyt-owl`, `week11-football`, `week11-tur-owl` and the sample game, and of all three soccer matches. The four week 7 and week 8 football games are not recorded (they did not fit under the 15 MB cap) and use the browser's voice, as does any line without a recording. The kickoff and final jingles play. The lines and the recordings' coverage were checked by scripts. Nobody has checked the voice out loud on a demo device.
- Crowd and whistle sounds are made in code, with captions on screen, under a recorded crowd background (`js/screens/sounds/stadium-crowd.mp3`). The football stadium is the soccer stadium's code with a football field in it (pixel stands in both teams' colours, boards, mown grass); its fans do not move, as in soccer. Nobody has checked how they sound or run on a real device.
- The player names on both football rosters are real NFL players, and on both soccer rosters real footballers (21 of 22 found with the same position in ESPN's data; ESPN has no current record for William Saliba).
- The week 11 football game (`data/games/week11-football.json`, no longer the one the app opens on and not linked from the league) was built by the game maker (`engine/make-game.js`): a full legal game in which every player ends with exactly the target stat lines. `node data/checks/game-vs-lines.js` replays it through the rules and checks every player's catches, yards, touchdowns, passing, kicks by distance, both defenses and the fantasy points against the targets: all equal (final GOB 17, PYT 14, 84 plays). 40 of the 84 plays were chosen by Jev 1.14.0, an outside play caller reached over the network, which returned real probabilities; the others had one legal option or an even draw.
- The soccer match the app plays (`soccer/data/week11-real-soccer.json`, Ironside 1, Red Harbour 3) was built by the soccer match maker, with equal weights (Jev was not used), from real numbers: `soccer/data/week11-real-lines.json`, converted from `data/statlines/soccer-real-2026-11-08.json`, where each player's line is from his own league match between 2026-11-05 and 2026-11-08 (ESPN game logs, read 2026-11-14). Two numbers were changed so that the match maker accepts them, and the lines file says so: each keeper's saves were raised to the other team's shots on target that were not goals (Raya 2 to 6, Maignan 5 to 8), and one real assist was left out (Wirtz has a real goal and a real assist from different matches, and a player cannot assist his own goal). Every other number is real. The match itself, the order of events and who passes to whom are made up. Soccer is reachable from the sport switch in the app.
- The earlier soccer match (`soccer/data/week11-soccer.json`) is still in the repository, built from the invented lines in `soccer/data/week11-lines.json`; the app no longer plays it.
- The league scoring rules (`data/scoring.json`) say exactly what `js/rules.js` does: no point per reception, 1 per 25 passing yards, 4 per passing touchdown, field goals by distance, defense sacks and takeaways. `node agents/scout/check-scoring.js` checks the two agree.
- Season tendencies in `data/profiles/GOB.json` and `PYT.json`: real 2026 numbers read live from Sleeper's public API, turned into 0 to 1 numbers by formulas of our own, with the address and date next to every number. Recent form (`form`, the last three weeks each player played) is real in the same way. The lineup screen shows a hot, cold or steady mark from it with the source.
- `data/rosters/crosscheck-2026.json`: Sleeper's season numbers compared with ESPN's public JSON for all 14 football players. All 14 agree on every compared stat.
- Soccer profiles (`data/profiles/IRN.json`, `RHB.json`): real 2026-27 league numbers from ESPN's public JSON for 21 of 22 players, with address, date and formula per number. Saliba stays null.
- `data/statlines/week09-real.json` (football) and `data/statlines/soccer-real-2026-11-08.json` (soccer) hold real numbers read from Sleeper and ESPN. The soccer match the app plays is built from the soccer file (see above). The football games are built from the real plays, not from this stat lines file; `node data/checks/real-week.js` uses it to check their points.
- The database schema (`db/schema.sql`) was run on a real Tiger Data database by the boss: 10 statements, no failures, `plays` is a hypertable, both aggregates exist. The plays service (`server/`) saved and read plays there once.
- Everything else in `db/` and `server/` has run against a real database only on a plain PostgreSQL 16, not on Tiger Data: `sh db/migrate.sh --plain --seed` applies the schema, the migrations and the seed twice with no error, and `node server/test-db.js` (19 checks) pushes both games, reads every route, opts in, releases a week, counts a watcher and relays a watch party. That run found and fixed one bug (the plays route left out the soccer minute).
- The service's tests and stress test pass against a stand-in database: every route, both sports, bad input on every route, 200 requests at once (`node server/test.js`, `node server/stress.js`). `node data/checks/all.js` runs every offline check in one go.

- Real plays: `data/rosters/plays-2026.json` holds 3,177 real snaps of the 28 football roster players from ESPN's play-by-play (weeks 1 to 10, 116 games), each with down, distance, field position, depth, direction or run gap, yards and the game's address. 1,080 of 1,100 game lines equal ESPN's own box scores and 156 of 172 season totals equal Sleeper's; every difference is listed in the file.
- Tendencies from real plays (`play_tendencies` in each football profile, with sample sizes) and a scouting report for each of the 28 football roster players in `data/profiles/notes/`, where every claim links to the plays behind it. The reports were rewritten on 2026-11-14 in Juan's laptop session with Scout's tools, not by a model run of `scout.py`: the season numbers, recent form, cross-check and play-by-play were pulled again live (same numbers as before), each claim was written by hand from the splits the research tools returned, every cited play went through `verify_evidence` before `write_report` (140 claims, none dropped), and each report says who made it on its second line. Each football team also has a team profile (`data/profiles/<team>-team.json` and `.md`): how the offense moves the ball, who gets the ball in the red zone and on third down, who is hot or cold, with the tool call behind every row. The cited plays are examples (three to five a claim), so they show a pattern but do not prove its percentages. Scout's notes pages (`data/profiles/notes/<team>-<slot>.md`) come from fixed rules, not a model, and say so.
- A third football game, Turtles against Owls (`data/games/week11-tur-owl.json`, 24 to 20, 184 plays), built by the game maker from the invented stat lines in `data/statlines/week11-tur-owl.json`, with Jev choosing 97 of the 184 plays (rebuilt on Juan's laptop on 2026-11-15, model `jev-1.14.0`; the file's `report` says so); `node data/checks/game-vs-lines.js` replays it and compares every number the file states, both defenses and the score. Its touchdowns, turnovers and gains of 20 yards or more are tagged as highlights. Carries and field goal distances are not in the file, so the maker chose them.
- Service routes beyond the plays: `GET /games/:id/momentum` (buckets the fantasy awards in the database with `time_bucket`, or `date_bin` on a plain PostgreSQL, and says which in `engine`), `GET /players/:id/trend` (weekly points, change and hot streak from a weekly continuous aggregate, or from the game files with no database) and `GET /scout/:player` (the stored report with the plays behind each claim; `POST /scout/:player/refresh` runs Scout only when a model key is in the service's environment). Tested with a fake database and, for the SQL, on the plain PostgreSQL above.
- Two week 9 games (`data/games/week09-gob-tur.json`, `week09-pyt-owl.json`) were built from real plays: every catch and run in them is a rostered player's real week 9 play with the same yards (checked by `node data/checks/game-vs-lines.js`). Drive starts, incompletions and penalties are filler, and the quarterback's passing is only the catches by our own receivers. Both play to the final in the app, and the app opens on `week09-gob-tur`. The four week 7 and week 8 games are built the same way from those weeks' real plays and open from the league home.

## Simulated

- Every play of `week11-football` (built from the stat lines of the invented sample game in `js/data.js`, `--from-sample`), of Turtles against Owls `week11-tur-owl` (invented stat lines) and of the sample game. Neither week 11 game is linked from the league.
- In the six real-play football games (weeks 7, 8 and 9), the matchups, the order of the plays, the drive starts, incompletions and penalties, the clock and the defenses are made up; each catch, run and kick of a rostered player is real. No game as a whole happened, and no score matches a real result.
- The soccer matches: in the real one, only the players' totals are real (two adjusted, see above), never the events; the earlier match and the sample are invented throughout.
- Every stat line in `data/statlines/week11.json`, `week11-tur-owl.json` and `week12.json`. The files say they are invented.
- There is no live data feed. Nothing reads a real fantasy league or a real game while the app runs. "Live" on a matchup means the replay is playing.
- The two managers, the league, its other matchup, past scores and standings are sample data (`data/league.json` says `invented: true`). There is one viewer and no accounts.
- The wait is simulated: nothing is hidden for a week, scores are hidden only until the replay ends.

## Written but not proven, or not connected

- The opt-in is kept in the browser. It is also sent to the service's `optins` table only when the page is opened with `?api=<service address>`; that was tested against a stand-in database, not the real one. The app plays from the game file and reads no plays from the database. With `?api=` it also reads `/league`, sends the opt-in and the "reached the final" count, and uses the commissioner routes and the watch party relay. It does not read the momentum, trend or Scout routes.
- UPDATE 2026-11-15, the laptop run on the real Tiger Data service (`db/runs/laptop-2026-11-15.txt`): `sh db/laptop.sh` applied the schema, the seed and migrations 002 to 004 there, started the service against it, pushed all 11 game files and read every route (health, plays, soccer plays, summary, top plays, drives, momentum, story, opt-ins, trend, Scout, league, commissioner, release, party). Two things did not hold: the momentum route answered with `date_bin`, not `time_bucket`, because the service's check for the function fails on Tiger Data (the same query with `time_bucket` named was run there directly and its buckets matched), and `node server/test-db.js` failed at the trend check because the weekly aggregate had no rows yet (the aggregates only show refreshed rows). Both were fixed on `main` the same night (the function is now found through `pg_proc`, and migration 005 makes the aggregates real-time), and the second run (`db/runs/laptop-2026-11-15-rerun.txt`) had no failed step: the momentum route answered with `time_bucket` on Tiger Data, and `node server/test-db.js` passed all 19 checks there (trend from the weekly aggregate, momentum equal to the JavaScript buckets, opt-in, release, watched, party, on the test's own rows). The summary route still came back empty for the main game and was not looked into. The app itself still plays from a file.
- Before that run, on Tiger Data these had not run: the seed file, the opt-in, health and push routes, migrations 002, 003 and 004 (soccer, commissioner, and the `fp_awards` hypertable with the weekly continuous aggregate), the story, top plays, drives, momentum and trend routes, and every `time_bucket` query, including the one behind momentum. The first real run's summary read came back empty because the aggregates had not refreshed yet, and the weekly aggregate will do the same. The certificate file option was only tested with a fake file. The connection string was tried from this build environment and could not be reached, so none of this ran there.
- The watch party relay (`/party` routes, a phone as the remote) is in the service, in memory only, and a screen (`js/screens/party.js`) uses it with `?api=`; without it the screens fall back to a browser channel on one device. It passes its tests and has not been tried with two real devices.
- The announcer can remark on a player's tendencies and on hot or cold form, and the game view now loads the profiles and passes them to it. Nobody has counted how often it says them in a real game. `pitch/BROADCAST.md` is written by a script that passes them.
- No game's plays were shaped by the players' tendencies in the profiles. The real-play games take each play as it really happened; the week 11 game was built before the profiles were filled.
- The real soccer lines could not become a match exactly as they are: the players come from different clubs, so real keeper saves do not equal the other team's saved shots. They became one after the two changes listed above (the keepers' saves and one assist).
- The Scout agent (deepagents) ran once with a model, on 2026-11-15: one player (Nacua), `gemini-3.5-flash-lite`, about 2 minutes. Its report passed the evidence check but was weaker than the existing one, so it is kept only in `agents/scout/runs/`; its profile step also dropped data, which is now fixed. The other 27 players have not been run through it, and the Claude path has never called the API. The agent is a team (a main agent with stats, film-room and fact-checker subagents) that also runs end to end on a scripted stand-in model (`agents/scout/check_agent_team.py`), and its report writer refuses any cited play that is not real. Its tools ran live, without a model, through fixed scripts (`fill_profiles.py`, `form.py`, `statlines.py`, `crosscheck.py`, `fill_soccer.py`). `sh agents/scout/run.sh` is the one command for a real run.
- ElevenLabs: the lines were rendered on Noah's account (see above). A line or a game added after the last render has no recording and is spoken by the browser's voice until `js/announcer/voice/render.js` is run again. The player now fetches the current list of recordings past the browser's cache, so an old cached list no longer sends recorded lines to the browser's voice.
- Current team and roster status of every real player was not checked beyond what Sleeper and ESPN report.
